# TriggerFish Elements 2.4.0

## Room Reverb and Scene Pack 4

This release adds a spatial room reverb built around source and listener
positions in a rectangular room.

- Room Reverb accepts up to eight source channels. Each connected source has
  an independent X/Y position in the room view, and the listener can also be
  moved in two dimensions.
- The direct field uses source-to-listener direction and distance to produce
  the stereo image. It remains sample-aligned with the input; Pre Delay applies
  to the wet room paths.
- Early reflections are generated from rectangular-room image sources and a
  two-ear listener model. Room size, aspect, source position, and listener
  position therefore shape their timing and stereo direction.
- The late field uses a two-stage, 16-line velvet feedback network with
  frequency-dependent decay, fractional-delay modulation, stereo width, and
  optional shimmer. Low Cut and High Cut shape the wet output.
- The ER / Tail control moves around the spatially inferred early/late balance,
  while Mix controls the complete dry/wet blend.
- Medium Hall is the default scene. Small Room provides a short natural
  ambience, and Superlush provides a darker, highly diffused modulated wash for
  sustained and synthetic material.
- Scene Pack 4 combines four mono or polyphonic source bundles into one cable.
  Room Reverb expands the connected channels into separately placeable room
  sources, up to its eight-source limit.
- A self-contained technical report documents the geometry, direct and early
  fields, velvet diffusion, feedback network, coefficient sets, decay filters,
  modulation, shimmer, and output filtering.

## Prog Sequencer and Transport

Prog Sequencer enters beta in 2.4.0. Its syntax, semantics, and feature set are
subject to change during the beta period. Its text language is inspired by
TidalCycles, Gibber, trackers, and step sequencers, with an emphasis on quickly
typing and reshaping musical material.

- Programs can define several independent sequences and arrange complete
  sections into a song. Sequences support scale degrees, named pitches,
  registers, chords, voicings, rests, ties, slides, accents, ghosts, ratchets,
  Euclidean rhythms, probability, seeded random values, and structural or
  timing transforms.
- Auxiliary lanes can cycle at their own lengths and rates. The `...` form
  aligns selected values with the beginning, end, or both edges of each Notes
  pass, and semicolons provide visual grouping without changing timing.
- Musical time values use familiar forms such as `4n`, `16n`, `8nd`, and
  `8nt`. Bare timing values remain available for quarter-note beat arithmetic.
- Three monophonic CV lanes support stepped, linear, smooth, and power
  interpolation. Each lane can generate an AD, AR, or ADSR envelope, either as
  its complete output or added to a CV sequence, with optional Velocity and
  Accent scaling.
- The editor shows active musical terms, arrangement position, clock progress,
  and compact scrolling CV traces. Positive, negative, and bipolar traces place
  their zero axes appropriately. Perceptually uniform and monochrome CRT-style
  heatmaps are available from the context menu.
- Text editing participates in Rack undo and redo. Shortcuts cover statement or
  document evaluation, local sequencer control, and the connected global
  transport.
- Transport supplies one precise 24 PPQN clock, shared Run state, and Reset
  pulse. Restart, Pause, Play, and Stop keep any number of connected Prog
  Sequencers sample-aligned from a common master.
- The reference manual presents the language progressively, followed by a
  complete formal grammar and shortcut reference.

## Polyphony and existing modules

- Slop now follows up to 16 input channels. Hum is shared across the instrument,
  while each channel has an independent drift process.
- Slop 4 now handles four polyphonic oscillator paths. Common thermal drift and
  hum remain coherent across the instrument, with independent drift for every
  voice and oscillator path.
- VDPO and VCA now support up to 16 channels, including independent nonlinear,
  oversampling, and filter state for each voice. Monophonic modulation signals
  are broadcast across the active channels.

## Patches, documentation, and development

- Added a synchronized musical Room Reverb patch with slow bass and folded
  arpeggio sources, sequenced cutoff and fold modulation, Scene Pack placement,
  and the Superlush scene.
- Added a Prog Sequencer version of the 303 smoke patch while retaining its
  established oscillator and voice settings.
- Added editable panels, rendered module previews, topology and language
  documentation, and regression coverage for the new modules and DSP paths.
- Standardized Windows plugin and Python-extension builds on the MinGW toolchain
  used by the Rack SDK.
- The repository-local `uv` environment now installs the Python development and
  test dependencies from the lock file. The PyTorch reverb-analysis suite
  remains an optional dependency group.
- Continuous integration builds the plugin on Linux, macOS Intel, and macOS
  ARM, and runs the native DSP and Python test suites.

## Compatibility

TriggerFish Elements 2.4.0 targets VCV Rack 2 and retains the existing module
slugs and patch parameter IDs. Existing patches continue to load directly, and
the expanded Slop, Slop 4, VDPO, and VCA implementations preserve their
monophonic operation. Room Reverb, Scene Pack 4, Transport, and Prog Sequencer
are new module slugs in this release.

Prog Sequencer's source language and feature set are in beta and are subject to
change as live sequencing workflows are refined.
